# YC Application Q&A Analysis

**Date:** 2026-07-24
**Purpose:** Analyze YC application questions and patterns in successful applicants' answers

---

## Key YC Application Questions (15)

### Part 1: Basic Information

**Q1. What is your company going to make?**
- Describe the product in one sentence
- The more specific, the better
- "AI platform"  → "AI that auto-fills customs forms for Korean exporters"

**Q2. Why did you pick this idea to work on?**
- Personal connection
- Market insight
- "Why now?" timing

**Q3. What will you do first if we give you money?**
- Plan for the first 3 months
- Concrete milestones
- MVP + securing first customers

---

### Part 2: Team

**Q4. Who are the founders?**
- Names, roles, backgrounds
- Emphasize core strengths

**Q5. How long have you known each other?**
- Depth of the relationship
- Shared experiences
- Proof of trust

**Q6. What are your roles?**
- Clear division of responsibilities
- Complementary skills
- CEO/CTO distinction

**Q7. Are you open to relocating to San Francisco?**
- YC is SF-based
- "Yes" is the right answer
- If there are visa issues, explain them

---

### Part 3: Market & Product

**Q8. Who are your users?**
- Concrete personas
- Market size
- Approach/channel

**Q9. What's new about what you're making?**
- Differentiation
- What's different from existing solutions
- Technical edge

**Q10. What are your competitors?**
- Honestly acknowledge competitors
- Points of differentiation
- Avoid "we have no competitors"

**Q11. How will you make money?**
- Business model
- Pricing strategy
- Unit economics

---

### Part 4: Traction & Progress

**Q12. What have you built so far?**
- MVP, prototype
- Demo link
- Technical achievements

**Q13. Do you have revenue?**
- Exact figures
- MRR/ARR
- Growth rate

**Q14. How many users do you have?**
- Active user count
- Growth trend
- User feedback

**Q15. What's the most impressive thing you've done?**
- Side projects
- Technical challenges
- Founding experience

## Patterns in Successful Applicants' Answers

### 1. "What is your company going to make?"

**Good answer:**
```
"We're building AI that auto-generates customs
documentation for cross-border traders in Asia.
Currently, exporters spend 8+ hours per shipment
on paperwork. We cut it to 10 minutes."
```

**Pattern:**
- A concrete problem (customs documentation)
- A clear target (cross-border traders in Asia)
- The pain point quantified (8 hours → 10 minutes)
- Avoid being abstract ("AI platform for trade")

---

### 2. "Why did you pick this idea?"

**Good answer:**
```
"I'm from Korea and served in military logistics.
I saw firsthand how painful cross-border paperwork
is. My co-founder studied accounting and
built fintech tools. We realized this is a $300B
problem with no good digital solution in Asia."
```

**Pattern:**
- Personal experience (military logistics)
- Team connection (co-founder's accounting background)
- Market size ($300B)
- "Why us" (unfair advantage)

---

### 3. "What will you do first?"

**Good answer:**
```
"Month 1: Interview 50 Korean exporters, build
waitlist. Month 2: MVP for one document type
(commercial invoice). Month 3: Beta with 10
customers, iterate based on feedback."
```

**Pattern:**
- Concrete milestones (50 interviews, 10 beta customers)
- A staged approach (starting with one document type)
- Customer-centric (iterating on feedback)
- Avoid being vague ("build the platform")

---

### 4. "Who are your users?"

**Good answer:**
```
"Primary: Korean SME exporters shipping to
Southeast Asia (50,000+ companies). Secondary:
Freight forwarders who handle documentation for
multiple clients. We'll reach them through KOTRA
(Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency)
partnerships and trade associations."
```

**Pattern:**
- A concrete segment (Korean SME exporters)
- Market size (50,000+ companies)
- Go-to-market (KOTRA partnerships)
- Secondary market (freight forwarders)

---

### 5. "What's new about what you're making?"

**Good answer:**
```
"Existing solutions like Descartes and Flexport
focus on US-Europe routes and cost $500+/month.
We're building for Asia-specific regulations
(Korean customs, ASEAN import rules) at $50/month.
Our AI is trained on 10,000+ Korean trade documents."
```

**Pattern:**
- Named competitors (Descartes, Flexport)
- Clear differentiation (Asia-specific, pricing)
- Technical edge (trained on 10,000+ documents)
- Pricing strategy ($50 vs $500)

---

### 6. "What's the most impressive thing you've done?"

**Good answer:**
```
"I built a side project that auto-translates
Korean web novels into English using LLMs. It
gained 5,000 users in 3 months with zero marketing.
I also led a military logistics team of 20 people,
managing $2M in equipment across 5 bases."
```

**Pattern:**
- Concrete results (5,000 users, 3 months)
- Proof of technical skill (LLM side project)
- Leadership (military logistics team)
- Impact quantified ($2M in equipment)

## Special Tips for Idea-Stage Teams

### What to emphasize when you have no traction

**1. Fast execution**
```
"I built an MVP in 2 weeks using [tech stack].
It's not perfect, but it works. Here's the demo:
[link]"
```

**2. Market research**
```
"I interviewed 30 potential customers. 25 said
they'd pay $X for this solution. Here are their
names and companies."
```

**3. Technical achievements**
```
"I published a paper on [topic] at [conference].
I also have 3 patents pending in [area]."
```

**4. Relevant experience**
```
"I worked at [company] where I saw this problem
firsthand. I know the market because I lived it."
```

## Tips for International (Korean) Founders

### 1. Be clear in English
- Avoid complex Korean-specific concepts
- Frame things in a context YC partners can understand
- "Military service" → "military service, led logistics team"

### 2. Sell the Korean market
- Korea is a perfect testbed
- Small but cutting-edge
- Potential for global expansion

### 3. Visa/relocation plan
- Be clear about your SF relocation plan
- Be upfront if there are visa issues
- "We'll figure it out"  → give a concrete plan instead

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Too abstract
 "We're building an AI platform for businesses"
 "We're building AI that cuts customs paperwork from 8 hours to 10 minutes for Korean exporters"

### 2. Exaggerating market size
 "This is a $1 trillion market"
 "There are 50,000 Korean SME exporters spending $2B annually on documentation"

### 3. Dismissing competitors
 "We have no competitors"
 "Flexport does this for US-Europe at $500/month. We're Asia-specific at $50/month"

### 4. Unclear team roles
 "We both do everything"
 "I'm CTO building the AI. My co-founder is CEO handling sales and partnerships"

### 5. Overstating traction
 "We have tons of users"
 "We have 50 beta users, 10 paying $50/month"

2. **A personal connection** — why this problem
3. **Fast execution** — MVP, demo, waitlist
4. **Honesty** — be upfront if you lack traction
5. **Team synergy** — why you two
