## 5. Overall Takeaways

1. **What you built matters more than where you went.** Most bios lead with a previously built product's traction (ARR, users, an exit) rather than a school name.
2. **Non-elite school + hands-on experience is common.** Cases like UC Davis dropout, or college dropouts who instead emphasize direct work experience (Capital One, Apple, etc.), show up repeatedly — lack of a prestigious degree doesn't seem disqualifying if there's concrete execution to point to.
3. **Second (or third) founding attempts are common.** Phrases like "2nd time founder" and "serial entrepreneur" recur — a prior failure or exit is framed as a strength, not a liability.
4. **Hardware/deep-tech backgrounds are not a small minority.** SpaceX, Tesla, NASA, Relativity Space, aerospace PhDs — this isn't an all-software cohort; a meaningful share have worked on physical systems.
5. **National/regional diversity.** Founders from Korea (Cuckoo Labs), Morocco (ZeroEntropy), Sweden (Vantel, Vetnio), Israel (Closure), and others explicitly foreground country-specific career markers (local military service, local Big Tech experience) as part of their identity.
