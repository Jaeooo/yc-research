## 4. Success Patterns

### 4.1 What Got Idea-Stage Companies Accepted

**Team signals that worked:**
1. **Elite school + domain expertise** — MIT/Stanford/Berkeley founders with relevant research or industry experience
2. **Ex-big tech, no exit** — Google/Meta/Amazon engineers leaving to build (48 Google, 29 Meta, 27 Amazon alumni)
3. **Serial entrepreneurs** — 269 founders with prior startup experience (even without exits)
4. **Dropout narrative** — 37 dropout founders; YC loves the commitment signal
5. **PhD + commercial application** — 59 PhD founders applying deep research to real problems
6. **2-person technical teams** — 237 two-founder companies; the sweet spot for idea-stage

**Industry patterns:**
- **B2B dominates** — 223 of 400 idea-stage companies (56%)
- **Healthcare + Industrials** — high idea-stage rates (60%+) suggest YC sees opportunity in these "hard" sectors
- **AI-everything** — even non-AI companies frame their value prop around AI capabilities

### 4.2 What Succeeded After YC

**Companies that stayed active showed these traits:**
1. **Found a specific vertical** — Not "AI for everything" but "AI for EMS billing" (Amby Health), "AI for construction supply chain" (Mercura)
2. **Technical depth** — Companies with PhD founders or deep research backgrounds built defensible products (Silurian's weather model, Mecha Health's radiology models)
3. **Pivoted fast when needed** — Admyral (real estate → voice AI), Zenbase (dev tools → scientific synthesis)
4. **Hardware + software combo** — Sensei (exoskeleton + marketplace), Piggy Robotics (humanoid robots)
5. **Clear revenue path** — B2B companies with obvious enterprise customers (Delineate with pharma, Agentin with Salesforce integrations)

**Companies that struggled:**
- Lumona: consumer search engine, crowded market, no clear moat
- Companies with vague one-liners or broad market definitions

## 5. Lessons for Our Team

### 5.1 Encouraging Signals

1. **Idea-stage acceptance is surging** — 74% of W25 was idea-stage. YC is explicitly betting on teams before product.
2. **Non-elite-school founders DO get in** — While MIT/Stanford dominate, there are founders from IIT Bombay, ETH Zurich, Oxford, Cambridge, NUS, TUM, and many other schools. SNU EE is comparable to these.
3. **2-person teams are the norm** — 237 two-founder idea-stage companies. Our 2-person setup is the most common accepted format.
4. **Military background is not a disqualifier** — Several founders had unconventional paths (gap years, military, career changes)
5. **Pivoting after acceptance is normal** — 17% of tracked companies pivoted significantly. YC invests in the team, not the initial idea.

### 5.2 Strategic Recommendations

**For the application:**
1. **Lead with technical depth** — Our SNU EE + Boston College CS combination shows strong technical fundamentals. Emphasize ability to BUILD, not just ideate.
2. **Pick a specific vertical** — Every successful idea-stage company had a razor-sharp one-liner. "AI for X" where X is specific and underserved.
3. **Show domain insight** — Amby Health founders had prior EMS software experience. Delineate's CEO had MIT PhD in bio + Pfizer/AstraZeneca. We need credible "why us" for our chosen market.
4. **Demonstrate builder instinct** — a0.dev founders were "serial app developers." Sensei built hardware prototypes before YC. Show we've already started building.
5. **Leverage the unique angle** — Korean military service → discipline, resilience, unique perspective on defense/govtech/operations. Boston College → US market access + accounting/finance domain knowledge.

**For post-acceptance:**
1. **Be ready to pivot** — 17% of idea-stage companies pivoted. The initial idea is a hypothesis, not a commitment.
2. **Go narrow first** — Every active company found a specific niche before expanding.
3. **Target enterprise customers early** — B2B companies have clearer revenue paths and higher survival rates.

## 7. Key Takeaways

### The Idea-Stage Playbook at YC (2024-2025)

1. **YC is increasingly idea-stage friendly** — The trend from 45% → 74% idea-stage over 3 batches is unmistakable. YC is betting more on teams than traction.

2. **The winning formula:** Small technical team (2-3) + elite skills (not necessarily elite school) + specific vertical + evidence of builder instinct.

3. **Pivoting is expected, not punished** — 17% of tracked companies pivoted significantly. The idea is an entry ticket, not a life sentence.

4. **B2B + AI is the dominant pattern** — 56% of idea-stage companies are B2B, most with AI at the core. This is where YC sees the most opportunity.

5. **Post-YC funding is rare early** — Only 2 of 18 tracked companies had confirmed external funding. Most are bootstrapping on YC's standard deal + early revenue.

6. **Hardware is back** — Robotics, industrial monitoring, and physical products are well-represented among successful idea-stage companies.

7. **International founders succeed** — ETH Zurich, Oxford, Cambridge, TUM, IIT, NUS founders all got in and are building successfully. SNU is in this tier.
